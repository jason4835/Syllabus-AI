COURSE DESCRIPTION

CHEM 104 is the second half of the general chemistry sequence. We begin
with intermolecular forces and solutions, spend the heart of the term on
kinetics and equilibrium, and close with thermodynamics, electrochemistry
and a short unit on nuclear chemistry. The laboratory runs in parallel
and is where most of the course actually happens.

Prerequisite: CHEM 103 (General Chemistry I) with a grade of C or better.

Textbook: Tro, Chemistry: A Molecular Approach, 5th edition, Chapters
11-20. Two copies are on reserve at Cabot Library.

Lab manual: Northbridge General Chemistry Laboratory Manual, Fall 2026
printing. Required, and you must bring it to every lab period.

LABORATORY

The laboratory meets every Tuesday afternoon in Priestley Hall 12,
starting in the second week of term with a safety and check-in session.
Seven of the sessions are formal experiments that you write up; the rest
are technique workshops and data-analysis periods with nothing to hand
in. Goggles and closed shoes are required, you will be sent home without
them, and a missed experiment cannot be made up in another section.

  Experiment 1  Solution preparation and colligative properties
  Experiment 2  Kinetics of the iodine clock reaction
  Experiment 3  Determination of an equilibrium constant
  Experiment 4  Acid-base titration and standardization
  Experiment 5  Buffer capacity and pH measurement
  Experiment 6  Solubility product of calcium hydroxide
  Experiment 7  Electrochemical cells and the Nernst equation

Each formal write-up follows the format in the manual: purpose,
procedure deviations, data, propagated uncertainty, discussion,
conclusion. Uncertainty analysis is worth a quarter of the grade on
every one of them, so do not leave it to the last paragraph.

LATE WORK POLICY

Homework sets may be submitted up to 48 hours late for 75% credit, and
your lowest homework score is dropped at the end of the term. Formal
write-ups are different: because the teaching assistants grade a whole
section at once, a report handed in after the deadline loses one letter
grade per day and is not accepted after four days. There are no late
submissions for prelab quizzes, since they are written in the room.
Exams are not rescheduled except for documented illness, religious
observance, or a university-sanctioned commitment, and a conflict you
can see coming must be reported to me at least one week ahead.

ATTENDANCE POLICY

Lecture attendance is worth 4% of the grade and is recorded through
in-class response questions on roughly two thirds of the meetings; you
may miss four of those with no penalty and no explanation. Laboratory
attendance is not optional. Sections are full and there is no capacity
to move you, so a missed experiment is a zero unless you arrange a swap
with the laboratory coordinator before the period begins. A student who
misses more than one experiment will not pass the laboratory component
and therefore will not pass the course.

ACADEMIC INTEGRITY

Discuss the homework freely. Work through the hard problems at the
whiteboard with anyone who will sit with you, then write your own
solutions from your own understanding. Laboratory data is a different
matter: you may share raw measurements with your bench partner, and you
must say so when you do, but the analysis, the error propagation and
every word of the discussion have to be yours. Fabricating or adjusting
data to fit an expected result is the most serious offense in this
course and is reported to the Office of Academic Integrity without
exception. Copying from a solutions manual, a past semester's write-up,
or a large language model and presenting it as your own is plagiarism.
Exams are closed-book; a provided equation sheet is the only aid
permitted.
