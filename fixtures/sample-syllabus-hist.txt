COURSE DESCRIPTION

HIST 310 traces Europe from the collapse of the old regime in 1789 to
the settlement of 1989 and its unravelling since. We move through
revolution, industrialization, nationalism, empire, two world wars, the
Holocaust, and the long shadow of the Cold War, always asking how
ordinary people experienced changes that textbooks describe from above.

This is a writing course as much as a history course. You will produce
roughly forty pages of prose over the term, most of it revised at least
once, and the research essay is built in stages so that no one arrives
at December with a blank page.

Prerequisite: HIST 101 or HIST 102, or permission of the instructor.

Textbook: Merriman, A History of Modern Europe, 4th edition, Volume 2.
Available at the campus bookstore and on reserve at Cabot Library.

A coursepack of primary sources is posted on Canvas. Bring the week's
documents to seminar, on paper or on a screen; we work from them
directly and you cannot participate without them.

GRADING

Your final grade is computed from the following components:

  Response Papers (5)                   20%
  Research Paper                        30%
  Midterm Examination                   15%
  Take-Home Final                       20%
  Class Participation                   15%
                                       ----
  Total                                100%

Grading scale: A 93-100, A- 90-92, B+ 87-89, B 83-86, B- 80-82,
C+ 77-79, C 73-76, C- 70-72, D 60-69, F below 60. I grade writing on
argument first, evidence second, and prose third, and I say so on every
rubric so that you know where the points went.

The Research Paper grade is split evenly across its three stages: the
proposal, the draft, and the revision. The revision is the version I
grade as a finished essay, and it is where most of the improvement
between a B and an A happens.

Class Participation means talking in seminar and in Friday discussion
section, having read the documents, and listening well enough to build
on what someone else just said. Silent attendance earns roughly half
these points.

LATE WORK POLICY

Response papers lose their purpose once we have discussed the documents,
so a response handed in after seminar has met earns at most half credit
and none at all after that week. You may skip one response with no
explanation and no penalty; the category is scored out of five, and
skipping a second one costs you the whole four points. Research paper
stages lose a third of a letter grade per day, which is survivable once
and expensive twice. Nothing is accepted after the take-home deadline,
because grades are due to the registrar the following morning. If
something serious is happening in your life, write to me before the
deadline rather than after it, and I will be far more flexible than this
paragraph sounds.

ATTENDANCE POLICY

This course does not work if you are not in the room, and attendance is
folded into the 15% participation grade rather than tracked separately.
Two absences across seminar and discussion section cost nothing and need
no explanation. Beyond that, each absence reduces the participation
component by roughly a fifth. Missing the midterm without prior
arrangement is a zero; make-ups are scheduled only for documented
illness, religious observance, or university-sanctioned travel, and a
foreseeable conflict must reach me a week in advance.

ACADEMIC INTEGRITY

Historical writing is an argument you make from evidence you cite, so
citation is not a formality here, it is the work. Use Chicago notes,
cite every quotation and every borrowed idea, and quote sparingly. You
are welcome to trade drafts with a classmate and to bring anything to
the Writing Center; both are collaboration I encourage, and neither
needs to be disclosed. What you may not do is submit prose you did not
write, whether it came from another student, a published historian, an
essay mill, or a large language model. Undisclosed use of generative AI
to produce text or to invent citations is plagiarism and is reported to
the Office of Academic Integrity. If you want to use such a tool to
brainstorm or to check your grammar, ask me first and we will agree on
what that looks like.

ACCESSIBILITY AND ACCOMMODATIONS

Northbridge University is committed to full participation for students
