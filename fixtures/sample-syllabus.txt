COURSE DESCRIPTION

MATH 221 extends single-variable calculus to functions of several
variables. Topics include vectors and the geometry of space, partial
derivatives, multiple integrals, vector fields, line and surface
integrals, and the theorems of Green, Stokes, and Gauss. The course
closes with a modeling project in which teams apply vector calculus to
a physical or data-driven problem of their choosing.

Prerequisite: MATH 122 (Calculus II) with a grade of C or better.

Textbook: Stewart, Calculus: Early Transcendentals, 9th edition.
Chapters 12-16. A copy is on reserve at Cabot Library.

LATE WORK POLICY

Each student has four late days for the semester, which may be spent on
problem sets in whole-day increments and applied at your discretion
without asking permission. Once your late days are exhausted, problem
sets lose 20% of their earned score per calendar day and are not
accepted more than three days after the deadline. Late days may not be
applied to the project proposal, the project final report, or to any
exam. If illness, a family emergency, or a documented university
obligation prevents you from meeting a deadline, contact me before the
due date and we will work something out; retroactive extensions are
much harder to grant.

ATTENDANCE POLICY

Attendance is expected at all lectures and recitations and is worth 3%
of your final grade. I take a roll count at the start of each Friday
lecture. You may miss up to three lectures for any reason with no
penalty and no explanation; after that, each additional unexcused
absence reduces the participation component by one third. Missing an
exam without prior arrangement results in a zero for that exam. Make-up
exams are scheduled only for documented illness, religious observance,
or university-sanctioned travel, and must be requested at least one week
in advance when the conflict is foreseeable.

ACADEMIC INTEGRITY

All work you submit must be your own. You are encouraged to discuss
problem sets with classmates and to work at the whiteboard together, but
you must write up your final solutions independently and without a
shared draft in front of you. List the names of everyone you worked with
at the top of each submission; collaboration is not a violation, but
undisclosed collaboration is. Copying solutions from a classmate, a
solutions manual, Chegg, or a large language model and presenting them
as your own is plagiarism and will be reported to the Office of Academic
Integrity. Exams are closed-book and closed-note; a single handwritten
formula sheet is permitted on both midterms and on the final.

ACCESSIBILITY AND ACCOMMODATIONS

Northbridge University is committed to full participation for students
with disabilities. If you have an accommodation letter from Student
Accessibility Services, please send it to me within the first two weeks
of the term so we can arrange exam timing and any other supports well
before Midterm Exam 1.
